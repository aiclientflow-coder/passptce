# Patient Safety & Quality Assurance — PassPTCE Original Study Notes

Domain weight: ~24% of PTCE (2026 outline). This domain rewards systems thinking: prevent errors before they reach patients, catch near misses, and improve processes.

## Medication error concepts

- **Error**: any preventable event that may cause or lead to inappropriate medication use or patient harm.
- **Near miss / close call**: error caught before reaching the patient — still report; gold for learning.
- **Adverse drug event (ADE)**: harm from medicine; may or may not be preventable.
- **Adverse drug reaction (ADR)**: harmful/unintended response at normal doses — report through site channels / MedWatch awareness.

Technician mindset: slow down at high-risk steps; verify product against label and Rx; escalate ambiguity.

## LASA — look-alike / sound-alike

Classic pairs used in teaching (examples, not exhaustive):
- hydrALAZINE vs hydrOXYzine
- predniSONE vs prednisoLONE
- HumaLOG vs HumuLIN
- NovoLOG vs NovoLIN
- CeleXA vs CeleBREX
- ZyPREXA vs ZyrTEC
- morphine vs HYDROmorphone (potency difference is critical)
- metFORMIN vs metroNIDAZOLE (different ends, still confused in busy settings)

Strategies:
- Tall man lettering (mixed-case to highlight differences)
- Separate storage bins
- Barcode verification
- Read back / repeat back for verbal orders where allowed
- Avoid abbreviating easily confused drug names

## High-alert medications (safety domain)

Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP) popularized high-alert lists. Core categories for exam recognition:
- Insulins
- Opioids
- Anticoagulants / antithrombotics
- Chemotherapeutic agents
- Neuromuscular blocking agents (institutional)
- Concentrated electrolytes (institutional)

Defenses: independent double-check where policy requires, smart pumps, standard concentrations, limited access, alerts that are meaningful (avoid alert fatigue).

## Error-prevention toolkit

1. **Barcode medication dispensing / scanning** — match NDC/package to selected Rx.
2. **Tall man lettering** — visual differentiation.
3. **Two-patient identifiers** — name + DOB (not room number alone in institutional settings).
4. **Independent double-check** — second person recalculates critical doses without anchoring on first person’s answer.
5. **Do-not-use abbreviations** — e.g., avoid U for units, QD/QOD confusion, trailing zeros on whole numbers, naked decimal doses (.5 vs 0.5).
6. **Clean as you go** — reduce mix-ups on the bench.
7. **One Rx at a time** in the workspace when possible.
8. **Flag unusual doses** to the pharmacist (pediatric, chemo, extreme frequencies).

## Quality assurance (QA) and continuous improvement

- QA checks: accuracy scans, pharmacist final check, secret shopper audits, inventory integrity.
- Root cause analysis (RCA): ask why repeatedly; fix systems, not only individuals.
- Continuous quality improvement (CQI): measure → intervene → remeasure.
- Culture of safety: reporting without fear of unfair punishment encourages near-miss capture.

## Infection control (tech scope)

- Hand hygiene before/after patient-facing tasks and compounding workflows.
- Personal protective equipment per USP and site policy for sterile / hazardous compounding.
- Distinguish **sterile** vs **nonsterile** compounding environments; do not pretend a clean counter equals an ISO cleanroom.
- Hazardous drugs (USP <800> awareness): receive, store, and handle with protection; spill kits.
- Sharps disposal in puncture-resistant containers; never recap toward hand.

## Sterile compounding awareness (not full compounder certification)

- Beyond-use dating depends on risk level and conditions — pharmacist/policy driven.
- Aseptic technique basics: garbing order, no cosmetics/jewelry per policy, first air principles.
- Techs may assist compounding only within training and law.

## Reporting pathways

- Internal: variance / incident report to supervisor/pharmacist.
- External awareness: FDA MedWatch for serious ADEs / product problems; vaccine programs have their own reporting (VAERS awareness).
- Board/quality programs vary by employer.

## Pediatric and elderly safety themes

- Weight-based dosing: verify units (mg vs mcg; mg/kg).
- Liquid measuring devices: oral syringes > kitchen spoons.
- Polypharmacy in elderly: more interaction and duplicate therapy flags for pharmacist review.
- Crushing restrictions: ER products, hazardous powders.

## Study tactics

- Memorize a dozen LASA pairs with tall-man pattern.
- Be able to define Class concepts for recalls (links to federal domain) and map them to pharmacy action: remove, quarantine, contact.
- Practice “what should the tech do first?” stems: usually stop, quarantine/segregate, notify pharmacist — not improvise therapy.
