# Federal Requirements Domain — PassPTCE Original Study Notes

Domain weight: ~19% of PTCE (2026 outline). Focus: Controlled Substances Act concepts, PSE limits, DSCSA, HIPAA, FDA recalls, DEA form *awareness*. This is federal technician knowledge — state law may be stricter; exams emphasize federal frameworks.

## Controlled Substances Act (CSA) basics

- DEA schedules I–V classify drugs by medical use and abuse/dependence potential.
- Pharmacies register with DEA; ordering, storage, inventory, and recordkeeping rules are stricter for controlled substances.
- C-II: no federal refills; partial fill rules exist in regulation/policy contexts — know concept that C-II handling is tightly controlled.
- C-III–V: limited refills under federal rules (commonly taught: up to 5 refills within 6 months for III/IV from date issued — verify current federal teaching points in your review course; memorize the *pattern* your PTCE prep emphasizes).
- Transfer of controlled prescriptions: more restricted than non-controls; C-II transfers generally not allowed like routine non-controls (one-time transfer concepts apply to certain III–V in federal teaching — follow current outline language).
- Valid prescription requirements: legitimate medical purpose, issued by authorized practitioner acting in usual course.

### DEA Form awareness (recognition, not diversion how-to)
- DEA Form 222: historically associated with ordering Schedule I/II controlled substances (electronic CSOS is modern parallel). Exam: know *what it is for*, not how to forge/abuse.
- Theft/loss reporting: significant loss or theft of controlled substances must be reported (DEA Form 106 concept).
- Inventory: controlled substance inventories on a required schedule; C-II often separated in recordkeeping practice.

## Pseudoephedrine (Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act)

Federal retail limits for PSE (commonly tested):
- Daily: 3.6 grams per purchaser
- 30-day: 9 grams (retail)
- Mail-order monthly limits differ (often taught as 7.5 g / 30 days) — know that mail-order is tighter than retail 9 g.
- Stored behind counter / locked; photo ID; logbook (or electronic equivalent); product packaging limits.

Technician role: verify ID, ensure log completion, refuse sales that exceed limits, involve pharmacist for edge cases.

## DSCSA (Drug Supply Chain Security Act)

Core ideas for techs:
- **Product tracing**: transaction information (TI), transaction history (TH), transaction statement (TS) concepts — pharmacies must be able to receive and maintain tracing data for prescription drugs in the supply chain.
- **Suspect product**: reason to believe counterfeit, diverted, stolen, intentional adulteration, or otherwise unfit.
- **Illegitimate product**: credible evidence that it is counterfeit, diverted, stolen, intentionally adulterated, or unfit for distribution.
- Response concepts: quarantine, investigate, notify trading partners / FDA as required — site SOPs detail exact steps.
- Serialized product identifiers on packages support verification.
- Authorized trading partners only.

Exam angle: DSCSA = supply chain integrity and tracing; quarantine suspect product and escalate — do not dispense suspect stock.

## HIPAA privacy (technician level)

- Protected Health Information (PHI): identifiers + health data.
- Minimum necessary: use/disclose only what is needed for the task.
- Patients have rights to access their records (pharmacy processes via policy).
- Do not discuss PHI in public areas; verify identity before counseling pickup conversations.
- Incidental disclosures minimized with reasonable safeguards (lowered voices, screen positioning).
- Breach awareness: unauthorized acquisition/access/use/disclosure — report per policy.

## FDA recall classes

- **Class I**: reasonable probability that use/exposure will cause serious adverse health consequences or death.
- **Class II**: may cause temporary/reversible health consequences; serious consequences remote.
- **Class III**: not likely to cause adverse health consequences.
- Pharmacy actions: identify stock, quarantine/remove, notify patients when directed by pharmacist/management, document.

## FDA vs DEA vs CMS (role recognition)

- FDA: drug approval, labeling, manufacturing quality, recalls, some OTC rules.
- DEA: controlled substances scheduling and controlled-substance registration/enforcement.
- CMS / payer rules: billing and coverage (often appear more in order entry / insurance questions).

## OTC vs Rx and behind-the-counter

- Rx-only (legend) drugs require a valid prescription.
- OTC can be sold without Rx but still have age/quantity/safety limits for some items.
- PSE is classic behind-the-counter example with federal log/ID rules.

## Recordkeeping & labeling federal themes

- Prescription labels must include required elements (patient, drug, directions, pharmacy identifiers, etc. — exact list is standard NAPLEX/PTCE teaching; know completeness matters).
- Aux labels support safe use but do not replace counseling.
- Beyond-use dates / expiration awareness for dispensed products per policy and USP concepts at tech level.

## What this domain is NOT

- Not patient-specific therapy advice.
- Not instructions for diverting controlled substances.
- Not state board law deep-dives beyond “states may be stricter.”
